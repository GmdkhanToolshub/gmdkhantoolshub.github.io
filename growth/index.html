Career guidance: six steps to your next job

  1. Be clear about your goal. Write the type of role, the industry and the location you want. A clear target makes every other step easier.
  2. List your strengths with evidence. For each skill, note one real example with a result, such as “reduced reporting time from two days to two hours”.
  3. Build a focused resume. Keep it to one or two pages, put results before duties, and match key words from the job advert honestly.
  4. Search in more than one way. Use job sites, company career pages, professional networks and people you know. Many roles are filled through recommendations.
  5. Prepare for interviews. Research the company, practise answers aloud and prepare two or three questions of your own.
  6. Follow up and learn. Send a short thank-you message after an interview, and note what you would improve for next time.

Professional communication tips

  • Lead with the point. Start emails with the request or conclusion, then give the detail.
  • Use clear subject lines. “Approval needed by Friday: office layout” is better than “Question”.
  • Keep it short. One idea per paragraph, and bullet points for lists.
  • Be specific about actions. Say who should do what, and by when.
  • Read it again before sending. Check names, numbers, attachments and tone.
  • Disagree respectfully. Focus on the issue, give your reason and offer an alternative.
  • Listen actively. Repeat key points back to confirm you understood.
  • Choose the right channel. Use a call for sensitive topics and writing for decisions you need on record.

Leadership and management tips

  • Set clear expectations. People do better when they know what good looks like.
  • Give feedback early and kindly. Describe the situation, the effect and what to do next.
  • Listen first. Ask for ideas before giving yours.
  • Delegate outcomes, not just tasks. Explain the goal and trust people to find the way.
  • Recognise good work in public. Be specific about what was done well.
  • Protect focus time. Reduce unnecessary meetings so the team can do deep work.
  • Admit mistakes. A leader who owns errors makes it safe for others to speak up.
  • Invest in growth. Hold regular one-to-one conversations about learning and goals.

Workplace productivity habits

  • Plan the day in the morning and review it in the evening.
  • Work in focused 25-minute blocks, then take a short break. Try the Pomodoro timer.
  • Keep one task list and one calendar, not many.
  • Group similar tasks, such as answering email, into set times.
  • Automate repeated work with templates and shortcuts. The Excel formula generator can help with spreadsheets.
  • Record what you learn in My Workspace so you can find it later.

This content is general guidance, not legal, financial or employment advice. Practices differ between countries and organisations.
