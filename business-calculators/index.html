GM Business Calculator Lab

Calculators for sales, stock and team measures. Each shows how the result is worked out, so you can explain the number to others.

Sales growth

Compare two periods. Add the number of periods to see the average compound rate per period.

Enter your figures to see the result.

How it is worked out

  • Change = later − earlier
  • Growth % = change ÷ earlier × 100
  • Compound rate per period = (later ÷ earlier)1 ÷ periods − 1

Example: Sales rise from 800,000 to 1,000,000: a change of 200,000, or 25%. If that took 2 periods, the compound rate is about 11.80% per period.

Inventory turnover

How many times stock is sold and replaced in a period, and how many days it stays on the shelf. Use cost values, not selling prices.

Enter your figures to see the result.

How it is worked out

  • Average stock = (opening + closing) ÷ 2
  • Turnover = cost of goods sold ÷ average stock
  • Days of stock = days in the period ÷ turnover
  • A two-point average can mislead if stock swings during the period; use monthly averages if you have them.

Example: Cost of goods sold 1,200,000, opening stock 180,000 and closing stock 220,000 over 365 days: average stock 200,000, turnover 6 times, about 60.8 days of stock.

Employee productivity

Output per hour and per person. Output can be units, orders, calls or sales value; use the same measure each time you compare.

Enter your figures to see the result.

How it is worked out

  • Output per hour = output ÷ total hours
  • Output per person = output ÷ number of people
  • Change % = (this output per hour − earlier) ÷ earlier × 100
  • A single number does not show quality, difficulty or conditions. Do not rank people on it alone.

Example: 2,400 orders, 480 hours and 3 people: 5 orders per hour and 800 per person. Against an earlier 4 per hour, that is 25% higher.

Attendance percentage

Attendance for one person over a period, with and without approved leave.

Enter your figures to see the result.

How it is worked out

  • Attendance % = days present ÷ working days × 100
  • Excluding approved leave % = days present ÷ (working days − approved leave) × 100
  • Days needed to meet the requirement = required % × working days, rounded up

Example: 26 working days, 23 present and 2 on approved leave: 88.46% of all working days, or 95.83% excluding leave. To meet 90% of all working days, 24 days are needed.

Questions

What counts as a good inventory turnover?

It depends on the trade. Fresh food turns far faster than furniture. Compare with your own earlier periods and with businesses selling similar goods.

Should approved leave count against attendance?

Organisations differ. The calculator shows both: attendance against all working days, and against working days after approved leave. Use the one your policy states, and show which you used.

Why is growth not shown when the earlier figure is zero?

A percentage change from zero cannot be calculated. Report the amount of the change instead.

Is anything I type sent to a server?

No. These calculators run in your browser and do not send or store what you enter.

Results are estimates from the figures you enter. They are not accounting or HR policy advice.
