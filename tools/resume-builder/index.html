What makes a resume ATS-friendly

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is software that employers use to collect and sort job applications. There are many different systems and each works differently, so no tool can honestly guarantee a pass. A clean format simply gives your resume the best chance of being read correctly by both the software and the recruiter who sees it next.

  • Use real text: text inside images may not be read at all. This builder creates a PDF with selectable text.
  • Keep to one column: side columns and tables can scramble the reading order, so these templates use a simple single-column layout.
  • Use standard headings: "Experience", "Education" and "Skills" are easier to recognise than creative labels such as "My Journey".
  • Match the job's wording: if the advert says "stakeholder management" and you truly have that skill, use the same phrase.

Our guide to ATS-friendly resumes covers this in more depth.

What to write in each section

Contact details

Include your name, phone number, a professional email address, your city and your LinkedIn link. A full street address is rarely needed.

Professional summary

Write two to four sentences covering your role, your experience, your strengths and one real result. Skip phrases like "hard-working team player", which any applicant could claim.

Project manager with 7 years in FMCG sales operations. Led distributor reporting across 3 regions and reduced monthly report preparation from 5 days to 2. Skilled in KPI tracking, Excel and cross-team coordination.

Replace the details with your own true figures. Never invent results, because interviewers will ask about them.

Experience

List your most recent job first, with your job title, employer and dates. Use about three to six bullets per role, and spend more of them on recent jobs. A helpful pattern for each bullet is action + task + measurable result:

WeakStronger
Responsible for sales reportsPrepared weekly sales reports for 12 managers, cutting errors by 30%
Handled customer complaintsResolved about 40 customer complaints a week, keeping 95% within 24 hours
Worked on recruitmentScreened 200+ applications and filled 8 roles in one quarter

The numbers above are only examples. If you don't have exact figures, a fair estimate or a scale such as team size, budget or number of customers still helps. Strong opening verbs include led, built, reduced, improved, negotiated, trained and launched.

Education

Give your degree, institution and year. If you have several years of experience, this section can be short. If you are a recent graduate, place more weight here and add relevant projects, internships or strong results.

Skills

List specific skills such as "Excel pivot tables", "Power BI" or "budget forecasting". Include those from the job advert that you can really demonstrate. Leave out basics that every employer assumes, such as using email.

Certifications and additional sections

Use this field for certifications, languages, volunteering or professional memberships. Include each item only if it supports the job you want.

How long should a resume be?

One page generally suits students and people with up to about ten years of experience. Two pages are normal for longer careers or senior roles. Some sectors, such as academia, expect longer documents. Relevance matters more than a strict page limit, so cut older or unrelated details first. If you are slightly over a page, try the Compact template.

Applying for jobs in Pakistan and abroad

Expectations differ by employer and country, so follow the job advert first. Some general guidance:

  • Protect your identity: leave out your CNIC number, and don't share copies of ID documents until a verified employer needs them for hiring.
  • Personal details: details such as father's name, religion, marital status and date of birth are often unnecessary. Include them only if the employer asks.
  • Photos: many employers in Pakistan and the Gulf are used to seeing photos, while employers in the US, UK and Canada generally prefer none. This builder creates a resume without a photo, which suits most online applications.
  • Language: the PDF export supports Western European characters, so names in Urdu or Arabic script need to be written in English letters.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sending the same resume to every job rather than adjusting it for each advert.
  • Listing duties without showing any results.
  • Spelling mistakes, inconsistent dates or an unprofessional email address.
  • Leaving unexplained employment gaps that a short, honest note could address.
  • Claiming skills you can't back up at interview.

Frequently asked questions

Will recruiters and ATS software read this PDF?

The PDF contains real text rather than images, with standard headings and no tables, which is the layout most systems parse best.

Where is my data stored?

Only in your browser's local storage on this device. Clear it any time with “Clear all”.

Can I use accented or non-Latin characters?

The PDF export supports Western European characters. Other scripts will be replaced with a question mark, so use the .txt download or print the preview for those.

Is this resume builder really free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, and the PDF and text downloads are free.

How long should my resume be?

One page generally suits people with up to about ten years of experience, and two pages are normal for longer careers or senior roles. Some sectors, such as academia, expect more.

Should I put my CNIC number on my resume?

No. Your CNIC number isn't needed to apply, and a resume can be passed to many people. Share ID details only when a verified employer needs them for hiring.
