How to use the Meeting Minutes Generator

  1. Enter the meeting title, date, attendees and optional agenda.
  2. Type your notes one point per line. Start lines with Decision:, Action:, Risk: or Next meeting: to place them.
  3. For actions add @Owner and “by date”, for example “Action: Share the plan @Ali by Friday”.
  4. Press Generate minutes, edit if needed, then copy or download as PDF.

About this tool

Minutes are most useful when they record decisions and actions, not a transcript. The tool sorts each line by its prefix, extracts an owner and a due date for actions, and flags actions that are missing either one, because an action without an owner or date is rarely completed.

Free to use. Your inputs are processed in your browser and are not sent to our servers. Results are estimates for general information; see the disclaimer.

Turn rough notes into minutes

Type or paste your notes, one point per line. The generator sorts them into sections based on how each line starts:

Start the line withWhere it goes
Decision:Decisions, numbered D1, D2…
Action:Action items, with an owner and a due date
Risk:Risks and issues
Next meeting:Next meeting details
Anything elseDiscussion points

For actions, add @Name for the owner and by plus a date for the deadline.

Example

These notes:

Sales were 94% of target in October
Decision: Extend the promotion to 30 November
Action: Share revised route plan @Ali by Friday
Risk: Stock shortage in two cities
Next meeting: Monday 10 am

become minutes with one discussion point, one decision (D1), one action (A1, owned by Ali and due Friday), one risk and the next meeting date. If an action has no owner or due date, the generator marks it TBC and reminds you to fill it in.

What good minutes include

  • The title, date, time and location or meeting link.
  • Who attended, and who sent apologies.
  • The agenda.
  • A short summary of the discussion, not a word-for-word record.
  • Clear decisions.
  • Action items, each with one owner and a due date.

Tips for faster minutes

  1. Write actions as you go: type "Action:" the moment someone agrees to do something.
  2. Use one owner per action: when two people own something, it often doesn't get done.
  3. Give a real date: "by Friday 13 November" is clearer than "soon".
  4. Send minutes the same day: people remember the details better, and actions start sooner.
  5. Review actions at the next meeting: start with the previous action list.

Download the minutes as a PDF with an action table, or copy the text into an email. You can draft the covering email with the Message Writer.

Frequently asked questions

How long after the meeting should minutes go out?

Within 24 hours is a common good practice while the discussion is still fresh.

What if I do not use prefixes?

Unprefixed lines become discussion points. You can still edit the generated minutes.

Does it store my notes?

No. Everything is processed in your browser.

How do I add an action with an owner and due date?

Start the line with Action:, add @Name for the owner and by followed by a date. For example: Action: Share route plan @Ali by Friday.

Can I download the minutes as a PDF?

Yes. The PDF includes a table of action items with owners and due dates.

Are my meeting notes uploaded?

No. The minutes are created in your browser, and your notes aren't sent to our servers.
