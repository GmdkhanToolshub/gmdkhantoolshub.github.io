How to use the Pomodoro Timer

  1. Choose Focus, Short break or Long break, or keep the default.
  2. Press Start (or the space bar) and work on one task.
  3. Take a break when the timer ends.
  4. After a few focus sessions, take a longer break.

About this tool

The Pomodoro Technique, created by Francesco Cirillo, uses short focused work sessions with regular breaks. Change the lengths in Timer settings; they are saved in your browser.

Frequently asked questions

How does the Pomodoro Technique work?

Work on one task for 25 minutes, take a 5-minute break, and after four sessions take a longer 15–30 minute break.

Will the timer keep running in another tab?

Yes. It uses the clock rather than counting ticks, so it stays accurate in the background. Turn on notifications to get an alert.

Can I change the session lengths?

Yes. Open Timer settings and set your own focus and break lengths.

How the Pomodoro technique works

The Pomodoro technique splits work into focused sprints, traditionally 25 minutes, followed by a 5 minute break. After four sprints you take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes. The timer keeps count so you do not have to watch the clock.

It works because a short, fixed block makes a big task feel manageable, and the breaks protect your attention. Many students use it for revision, and many professionals use it for writing, coding and email.

Practical tips

  • Pick one task before you start the timer and write it down.
  • Silence your phone for the 25 minutes. If a thought pops up, jot it on paper and return to the task.
  • If you finish early, review your work rather than starting something new.
  • Try 50 minutes of work and 10 minutes of rest if 25 feels too short for deep work.

Worked example

You have a report to write. Set a 25 minute focus session, work on only that report, and take a 5 minute break when the timer sounds. After four sessions, which is about two hours of work and three short breaks, take a longer 15 to 30 minute break. If a session is interrupted, note why and start a fresh one.
