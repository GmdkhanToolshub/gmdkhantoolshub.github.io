How to use the Calculator

  1. Type an expression or tap the keys. The answer appears as you type.
  2. Press Enter or = to keep the result and add it to your history.
  3. Switch to Scientific for functions, powers and angles in degrees or radians.
  4. Tap a history item to use it again.

About this tool

Calculations use a safe expression reader instead of running code. It follows normal order of operations, supports brackets, the % sign (which divides by 100), factorials, and multiplication written as 2π or 3(4+1). Results are shown to 12 significant digits.

Good to know: Computers store decimals approximately, so tiny rounding differences are hidden by showing 12 digits. Factorials are limited to whole numbers up to 170. The % key divides by 100 and does not work like the percent button on some handheld calculators.

Free to use. Your inputs are processed in your browser and are not sent to our servers. Results are for general information; see the disclaimer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage?

Type 15% of 200 as 200*15%, which gives 30. The % sign divides the number before it by 100.

What is the difference between degrees and radians?

They are two ways to measure angles. A full circle is 360 degrees or about 6.283 radians. Choose degrees for everyday angle problems.

Can I use the keyboard?

Yes. Type numbers and operators, press Enter to calculate and Escape to clear.

How your data is handled

This tool runs in your browser. What you enter is processed on your device and not uploaded to our servers.

Worked example

To work out 18% sales tax on an item priced at 2,500, type 2500*18% and the answer 450 appears as you type, so the total is 2,950. Press Enter to keep it in the history. Switch to Scientific to try something like sqrt(144)+2^3, which gives 20. Tap an old result in the history to reuse it in the next calculation.

Practical tips

  • Check percentage results once by hand, for example 10% of 200 should be 20.
  • Use brackets to control order, for example (4+6)*3.
  • Check whether Scientific mode is set to degrees or radians before using sin and cos.
  • Tap a history item to reuse an earlier result.
