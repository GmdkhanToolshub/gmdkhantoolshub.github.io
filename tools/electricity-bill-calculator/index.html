How to use the Electricity Bill & Units Calculator

  1. In Appliance units, enter how many of each appliance you run and for how many hours a day.
  2. Type your price per unit. A good number is your last bill total divided by its units.
  3. Read the monthly units, the estimated bill and which appliance costs the most.

About this tool

Your electricity bill is mainly units (kilowatt-hours) multiplied by a rate, plus fixed charges and taxes. Units for an appliance are its watts times the hours it runs, divided by 1000. Tariffs, slabs and taxes in Pakistan are set by the regulator and your distribution company and change from time to time, so this calculator asks for your own rate instead of guessing. Nothing is sent anywhere; it all runs in your browser.

Free to use. Your inputs are processed in your browser and are not sent to our servers. Results are estimates for general information; see the disclaimer.

How electricity units are calculated

One unit on your bill is one kilowatt-hour (kWh): a 1000-watt appliance running for one hour. The formula is units = watts × hours per day × days ÷ 1000.

Wattage on the label is the maximum. A refrigerator or an inverter AC does not draw full power all the time, so the calculator uses typical average values you can check against your own appliances.

Ways to cut your units

  • Air conditioner: it is usually the biggest cost. A higher thermostat setting and an inverter model use far fewer units.
  • Switch to LED: an LED bulb uses a fraction of the power of an old filament bulb.
  • Standby and idle: switch off chargers, TVs and computers you are not using.
  • Heavy appliances: run the iron, geyser and water pump only as long as needed.
  • Check the slab: where tariffs rise with usage, a few units less can lower the rate on the rest. Check this on your own bill.

Finding the right rate

Divide the total on your last bill by the units used to get an all-in average rate per unit, which already includes fixed charges and taxes. Use that number in both tabs for the closest estimate. For budgeting a household with other bills, try the Percentage Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many units does a 1.5 ton AC use?

A standard 1.5 ton AC drawing about 1500 W uses around 1.5 units for every hour it runs. At 8 hours a day that is roughly 360 units a month. An inverter AC often uses much less.

How do I calculate units from watts?

Multiply watts by hours of use and divide by 1000 to get units (kWh). A 100 W bulb running 10 hours uses 1 unit.

Why does my real bill differ from this estimate?

Real bills use tariff slabs, fixed charges, taxes and adjustments that change over time. Using the average rate printed on your own bill gets you closest.

Does this show current tariffs?

No. Tariffs change, so you enter the rate from your bill. That keeps the result right even after a tariff change.

Which appliance uses the most electricity?

Air conditioners and electric geysers usually do. The results table ranks your appliances by share of the total.

Worked example

Say you run two ceiling fans for 10 hours a day and one air conditioner for 6 hours a day. Enter each appliance with its hours, then type your price per unit, found by dividing your last bill total by its units. The tool adds up the monthly units, estimates the bill and shows which appliance costs the most. If the air conditioner tops the list, cutting its hours by one a day shows the saving straight away.

Practical tips

  • Work out your price per unit from your last bill: total divided by units.
  • Check each appliance's real wattage if the default looks wrong.
  • Cut the biggest appliance first, since small savings elsewhere add little.
  • Treat the result as an estimate and compare it with your printed bill.
