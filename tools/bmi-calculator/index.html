How to use the BMI Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter your weight and height.
  3. Tick the South Asian cut-offs if they apply to you.
  4. Read your BMI, category and healthy weight range.

About this tool

BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in m)². WHO adult categories are under 18.5 underweight, 18.5–24.9 healthy, 25–29.9 overweight and 30 or more obesity. A WHO expert consultation (2004) suggested lower action points of 23 and 27.5 for many Asian populations.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI does not measure body fat, so it can mislead for muscular people, older adults and during pregnancy. Use it as a screening guide only.

Why are there South Asian cut-offs?

Research suggests health risks rise at a lower BMI in many Asian populations, so a WHO expert group proposed lower cut-off points.

Can children use this?

No. Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific BMI charts. This calculator is for adults.

What your BMI does and does not tell you

Body mass index compares your weight with your height. It is a quick screening number, not a diagnosis. Adults with a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 are usually described as in the healthy range, 25 to 29.9 as overweight, and 30 or more as obese, while below 18.5 is underweight.

BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so a strong athlete can score high without carrying extra fat. It also does not show where fat is stored, and the ranges can fit different ethnic groups differently. South Asian adults often face higher health risk at lower BMI values, so a doctor may suggest a lower cut-off for you.

Practical tips

  • Measure height without shoes and weight in light clothing, at the same time of day.
  • Waist size is a useful second check. Ask your doctor what is right for you.
  • Use BMI to watch a trend over months, not to judge a single day.
  • This tool gives general information and is not medical advice.

Worked example

A person weighs 70 kg and is 170 cm tall. BMI is 70 divided by 1.7 squared, which is 70 / 2.89, or about 24.2. On the WHO scale that is in the healthy range of 18.5 to 24.9. With the South Asian cut-offs ticked, 24.2 sits just above the 23 action point, so the tool marks it as increased risk. Both readings use the same number; only the category lines differ.
