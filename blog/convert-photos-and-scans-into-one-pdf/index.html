Turn Photos and Scans Into One Clean PDF

Combine phone photos or scanned pages into a single, tidy PDF: how to take good photos, order the pages, choose a page size and keep the file small.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 20 November 20265 min read

Sooner or later you will need to turn a handful of photos into one PDF. Perhaps you have photographed a signed agreement, pages of a handwritten assignment, a set of receipts or a certificate with two sides. Sending five separate pictures is untidy and easy to misorder. A single PDF keeps the pages together, in order, and opens on any device. This guide shows how to take photos that convert well, how to build the PDF, and how to keep the final file a sensible size.

Start with good photos

The quality of the PDF depends mostly on the quality of the pictures. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Light. Use even light. Avoid a shadow from your phone or hand falling across the page.
  • Flat page. Place the paper on a flat, plain surface in a contrasting colour, and hold the phone directly above it so the edges are parallel and the text is not slanted.
  • Focus and distance. Fill the frame with the page without cutting off the edges, and tap to focus on the text.
  • Orientation. Take all pages the same way up. Mixed orientation causes sideways pages later.
  • One page per photo. Do not photograph two pages together unless they are meant to be one image.

Check each picture before you convert

Zoom in on every photo. Can you read the smallest text? Are any corners cut off? Is a thumb in the picture? It is much faster to retake a photo now than to discover a problem after the PDF has been sent. Rename the files in order, for example page-1, page-2 and page-3, so you can see the sequence at a glance.

Step by step with Images to PDF

  1. Open the free Images to PDF tool. The pictures are processed in your browser and are not uploaded.
  2. Drop your images in, or click to choose them. JPG, PNG and WebP are accepted.
  3. Use the arrows to put the pages in the right order. Each image becomes one page.
  4. Choose the page size, such as A4 or Letter, the orientation and a margin. A small margin keeps text away from the edge when the page is printed.
  5. Create the PDF and download it.
  6. Open it and scroll through. Confirm the page count and that no page is upside down.

Choosing page size and margin

A4 is the common size in Pakistan and most of the world, while Letter is used in North America. If the recipient will print the document, match the size they use. Portrait suits most letters and forms; landscape suits wide tables and certificates. Images are scaled to fit the page without stretching, so a picture with a different shape from the page will leave some white space, which is normal.

Keeping the file small

Photos from a modern phone are large, so a PDF of ten of them can run to tens of megabytes. Email systems and portals often limit attachments. There are three ways to keep the size down, and they work well together:

  1. Resize the photos before converting. A width of about 1600 pixels is usually enough for a readable page. See our guide on resizing images.
  2. Choose JPG for photographs of pages, since PNG files are much larger for the same picture.
  3. After you create the PDF, run it through the PDF Compressor on a medium setting, and check that the text is still sharp.

A worked example

You photograph a four-page rental agreement. Each photo is 3.5 MB, so the four pages come to 14 MB. The landlord's portal allows 5 MB. First, resize each photo to about 1600 pixels wide, which brings each to well under 1 MB. Convert the four pictures into an A4 portrait PDF with a small margin. Check the page order and open the file. If the PDF is 3 MB, you are done. If it is still above the limit, compress it once at a medium level, then re-open it and read the smallest clause to check it is legible.

Combining with existing PDFs

Sometimes some pages are already PDFs and others are photos. Convert the photos into a PDF first, then use PDF Merge and Split to join it with the other files in the right order. If a page ends up sideways, fix it with a PDF rotator before sending.

Is a photo PDF good enough for official use?

For many everyday tasks, such as sharing a signed document or submitting a form, a clear photo PDF is accepted. Some offices ask for an original scan, a certified copy or a particular resolution. If the instructions mention scanned documents, follow them exactly. Photographed pages are not searchable text; they are pictures of text. That is normal, but it means you cannot copy words from the PDF.

Privacy tips

  • Cover or leave out information the recipient does not need.
  • Delete the photos from your phone and cloud backups when you no longer need them, if they contain identity documents.
  • Use a browser-based tool so the files are not sent to someone else's server.
  • Name the final file clearly, and avoid putting an ID number in the file name.

Checklist

  • Take clear, flat, well-lit photos, all in the same orientation.
  • Rename files in page order.
  • Pick A4 or Letter to match the recipient.
  • Check the page count and orientation after converting.
  • Compress if the file is too big, then check readability.

Follow these steps and a stack of photos becomes one neat, professional document. Start with the free Images to PDF tool.
