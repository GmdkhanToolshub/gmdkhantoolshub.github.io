QR Codes for Small Businesses: 7 Practical Uses and Setup Tips

Seven ways small businesses can use free QR codes for menus, WhatsApp, reviews,directions and more, with setup steps, print sizes and mistakes to avoid.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 5 November 20265 min read

A QR code gives customers a quick way from something physical, such as a table, shop window or parcel, to something online. It costs nothing to create, and you don't need an app or a website to get started. A QR code won't bring in customers by itself, but it can remove a small step that might otherwise put someone off. Here are seven practical uses and how to set each one up with a free QR Code Generator.

1. A digital menu or price list

Restaurants, salons, tailors and repair shops change their prices often, and reprinting them is expensive. To set one up:

  1. Save your menu as a PDF, and keep the file small so it opens quickly on mobile data.
  2. Upload it to your website or to a file service such as Google Drive, and set it so anyone with the link can view it.
  3. Paste the link into the Website URL option and download the code.
  4. Test it on a phone that isn't signed in to your account, to confirm the file really is public.

Tip: keep a few printed menus available for customers who prefer them or whose phones can't connect.

2. A WhatsApp chat with one scan

If customers usually order or ask questions on WhatsApp, a code can open a chat with your business straight away. Use WhatsApp's click-to-chat link format, with your country code and without any plus sign, spaces or leading zero:

https://wa.me/92300XXXXXXX?text=Hello%2C%20I%20would%20like%20to%20place%20an%20order

Replace the X's with your number, and paste the link into the Website URL option. The customer sees your starting message and only needs to press send. A code like this works well on flyers, packaging, delivery bags and shop doors.

3. Google reviews

Reviews help new customers decide, but many happy customers won't go looking for the review page. Get your review link from your Google Business Profile, then turn it into a code for your counter, receipts or delivery packaging.

Ask everyone in the same way, and don't offer discounts or gifts for reviews. Google's review policies don't allow that, and it can put your listing at risk.

4. Directions to your shop

Finding a small shop can be hard, particularly in a busy market or on a side street. Open your location in Google Maps, select Share, copy the link and use the Website URL option. It's useful on business cards, flyers, wedding-hall or event invitations, and delivery notes.

5. A digital business card

The Contact (vCard) option saves your name, company, job title, phone number, email address and website directly to someone's phone. That spares them from typing your details, and they avoid mistakes in the number.

Add only the details you're happy to make public. A code with more details is also denser, so print it at least about 2 cm wide on a card and test it first.

6. Guest Wi-Fi

Cafés, waiting rooms and offices can save staff from repeating a password all day. Pick the Wi-Fi network option, then enter your network name, password and security type.

Important: the password is stored inside the code, and anyone who scans it can get it. Use a separate guest network, not the one that connects your payment devices or business computers.

7. Product details and reorders

A label only has room for so much. A code on your packaging can lead to care instructions, ingredients, a how-to video, your catalogue or a reorder page. To keep the link easy to update, point it to a page on your own website rather than directly to a file. Then you can change the page whenever you need to, without reprinting labels.

What size should you print?

A widely used guideline is that a person can scan from around ten times the width of the code. Treat these as starting points, and test a real print:

PlacementScan distanceSuggested width
Business cardAbout 20 cmAt least 2 cm
Table card or flyer30–50 cm3–5 cm
Counter or wall poster1–2 m10–20 cm
Shop window or bannerAbout 5 mAbout 50 cm

Download the SVG file for your printer, because it stays sharp at any size. If they ask for an image file, use the largest PNG size. Use a dark code on a light background, with the blank margin around it left in place.

Common mistakes

  • No instruction: a code alone doesn't say what it does. Add a short line such as "Scan to order on WhatsApp".
  • Links that need a login: a private file or a page only staff can see will stop customers right there.
  • Slow, heavy pages: a large PDF can be frustrating on mobile data. Compress any photos first with the Image Compressor.
  • Printing before testing: test a sample on an Android phone and an iPhone before you print hundreds.
  • Bad placement: avoid curved surfaces, glossy finishes that catch the light, and spots that are too high, too low or too dark to reach.
  • Depending on a temporary link: if the link stops working, so does every code you've printed. Choose addresses you control or expect to last.

Finding out whether it's working

The codes this tool makes are static, so they don't record scans. There are still simple ways to measure them:

  • Tagged links: if you link to your own site and use an analytics tool, add a tag such as ?utm_source=qr&utm_medium=poster to see visits from that code.
  • Separate messages: use a different starting message on each WhatsApp code, such as "Hello, I saw your flyer", to see where your chats began.
  • Just ask: a quick "How did you find us?" at the counter still works well.

A note on payments and security

This tool doesn't make payment codes. For Raast, bank or wallet payments, use the official code from your bank or payment provider, as a homemade link isn't a substitute for that.

Fake stickers placed over genuine codes are a known scam. Check your printed codes now and then, especially those in public places, and consider placing them behind glass or under lamination. Encourage staff and customers to check the address that appears before they enter any login or payment details.

Frequently asked questions

Are QR codes free to use for business?

Yes. The codes from this tool are free for business use as well as personal use, with no sign-up. You only pay for any printing.

Will my QR code stop working?

The code itself doesn't expire, as it holds the content directly. It keeps working for as long as the link or content it contains is still valid.

Can I change where a printed QR code goes?

Not with a static code. Instead, link it to a page on your own website, and update that page whenever you need to.

Do customers need a special app to scan it?

Most modern phones can read QR codes through the built-in camera. Some older phones may need an app, so keep key information available in another form too.
