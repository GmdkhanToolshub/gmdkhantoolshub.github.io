How to Calculate Percentage, Discount and Sales Tax

Simple formulas for percentages, price after discount, adding and removing sales tax, and percentage change, each with a worked example in rupees.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 10 November 20265 min read

On this page2 min read

  • What is X% of a number?
  • What percentage is one number of…
  • Price after a discount
  • Adding sales tax
  • Taking tax out of a total
  • Percentage increase or decrease

Percentages appear in prices, taxes, marks, salaries and discounts. A few formulas cover almost every case. Here they are, with examples you can check with a calculator.

1. What is X% of a number?

Amount = number × X ÷ 100. Example: 15% of Rs 8,000 = 8,000 × 15 ÷ 100 = Rs 1,200.

2. What percentage is one number of another?

Percentage = part ÷ whole × 100. Example: you scored 42 out of 60, so 42 ÷ 60 × 100 = 70%.

3. Price after a discount

New price = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100). Example: a Rs 8,500 jacket with 20% off costs 8,500 × 0.80 = Rs 6,800, so you save Rs 1,700.

4. Adding sales tax

Price with tax = price × (1 + tax rate ÷ 100). Example: at an 18% sales tax rate, Rs 6,800 becomes 6,800 × 1.18 = Rs 8,024. The standard sales tax rate in Pakistan is 18% at the time of writing, but some goods have different rates, so check the current rate for your product with FBR or your accountant.

5. Taking tax out of a total

If a bill of Rs 11,800 already includes 18% tax, price before tax = total ÷ 1.18 = Rs 10,000, and the tax is Rs 1,800. A common mistake is to take 18% off the total, which gives the wrong answer.

6. Percentage increase or decrease

Change % = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Example: a salary goes from Rs 80,000 to Rs 92,000. The change is 12,000 ÷ 80,000 × 100 = a 15% increase.

7. Two discounts are not added together

A 20% discount followed by a further 10% off is not 30%. Apply them one after the other: 100 × 0.80 × 0.90 = 72, a total discount of 28%.
