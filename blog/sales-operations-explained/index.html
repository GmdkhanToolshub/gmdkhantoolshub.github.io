What Does a Sales Operations Team Actually Do?

A plain-English guide to sales operations: planning, data, reporting, process and coordination, and why strong sales ops makes sales teams faster.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 5 November 2026Updated 26 November 20266 min read

On this page7 min read

  • From reporting to insight
  • Closing the loop

Sales teams are measured on results, but the machinery that helps them reach those results is often invisible. That machinery is sales operations: the planning, data, process and coordination work that lets a commercial team spend its time selling instead of chasing information.

The core of the role

Although titles vary between companies, the work can be grouped into five areas.

  • Planning and targets. Turning annual goals into monthly, regional and channel targets that are realistic and traceable.
  • Data and reporting. Collecting sales data from systems and distributors, validating it, and publishing reports leaders can trust.
  • Performance monitoring. Tracking KPIs such as achievement against target, coverage, and growth, and flagging gaps early.
  • Process and policy. Documenting how orders, claims, schemes and approvals work so they are consistent across regions.
  • Coordination. Connecting sales, finance, supply chain and marketing so decisions and actions do not fall between teams.

Why data accuracy comes first

A report is only useful if people believe it. When two teams report different numbers for the same month, meetings are spent debating the data instead of acting on it. Good sales operations teams define each metric once, document where it comes from, and reconcile differences before a number reaches senior management.

From reporting to insight

Reporting what happened is the starting point. The more valuable step is explaining why. Which territories are behind plan, which products are driving growth, and where is stock or coverage limiting sales? A simple variance table plus two sentences of commentary is often more useful than a dozen charts.

A useful report answers three questions: where are we against plan, why, and what do we do next?

Closing the loop

The final piece is follow-through. Decisions made in a review meeting need an owner and a date, and someone has to check they were completed. Tracking actions where everyone can see them makes repeated problems easier to spot.

Skills that help

Spreadsheet fluency, a good grasp of the commercial model, clear writing and the ability to work with people at every level are the common thread. Technical skills can be learned; reliability and attention to detail are what stakeholders remember.

How sales operations differs from sales management

The two roles work closely together, which is why they are often confused. A sales manager is accountable for the number: coaching the team, visiting customers and closing gaps. Sales operations is accountable for the system around that number: whether targets are fair, whether the data is right, and whether the process lets people act quickly.

Which customers should we visit this week?Sales management
How should the annual target split across regions?Sales operations, with sales leadership
Why does this report disagree with finance?Sales operations
How do we recover a territory that is behind plan?Sales management, using sales operations analysis

What a typical month looks like

Although every company has its own calendar, the work tends to follow a rhythm built around the monthly close.

Metrics worth tracking

A long KPI list dilutes attention. A team can start with a short set of measures, provided each one is clearly defined.

  • Achievement against target, in total and by region, channel and product group.
  • Growth against the same period last year. This reduces some seasonal distortion but does not remove all of it: holidays that move between months and a different number of selling days can still affect the result.
  • Coverage: how many of the target customers or outlets were actually served.
  • Productivity: for example the share of calls that resulted in an order.
  • Collections and returns, so that sales growth is not hiding a cash or quality problem.

Define each measure once

Before arguing about a number, write down how it is made. A one-line record for each measure is enough to start. These entries are examples.

A small variance table in Excel

Invented figures for one month:

Total5,000,0004,800,000−200,000−4.0%

With the headings in row 1, put region in column A, plan in B and actual in C. Enter these in D2 and E2, format E as a percentage, and copy both down:

=C2-B2
=IF(B2=0,"No plan",D2/B2)

If the actual figures come from a list of sales on a sheet named Sales (date in column A, region in B, amount in D), SUMIFS can total each region between a start date in H1 and an end date in H2. Use it in C2 and copy it down. If your dates include times, the end date needs care.

=SUMIFS(Sales!$D:$D,Sales!$B:$B,A2,Sales!$A:$A,">="&$H$1,Sales!$A:$A,"<="&$H$2)

Then add the two sentences a table cannot give you: why each large difference happened, and what will be done.

Signs a team needs stronger sales operations

The need often shows itself through everyday symptoms. Look out for regional managers keeping private spreadsheets, targets arriving after the month has started, review meetings that repeat the same actions, and senior leaders asking for numbers that take days to produce. These often point to a process problem. Check the process first, and give each issue a clear owner.

Getting started in the field

Accounts, sales administration and analyst roles can all be routes into sales operations. A practical way to prepare is to take a report you already produce and improve it: validate its inputs, document how each number is calculated, and add one sentence explaining what changed. That habit shows the mindset the role requires, and it is easy to demonstrate in an interview.

Frequently asked questions

Is sales operations the same as sales administration?

Job titles are used differently between companies. Compare the actual duties: administration often centres on processing, while the operations role described here also covers planning, data and process design.

Who should own the sales target figures?

Name one owner for the official target file, agree who approves changes, and keep a record of each change.

What is a sensible first improvement?

Pick one report that causes repeated arguments. Write down its definitions and sources, then reconcile it once from start to finish.
