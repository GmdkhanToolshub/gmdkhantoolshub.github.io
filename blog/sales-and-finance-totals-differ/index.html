Why Sales and Finance Report Different Totals

Find the difference between a sales report and the finance total: agree definitions, match invoices in Excel and build a bridge that explains every amount.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 27 November 20264 min read

Sales says the month closed at 1,250,000. Finance says 1,085,000. Both may be reporting correctly under their own rules, or one may contain a mistake. You cannot tell which from the two totals. You need to explain the difference line by line.

The practice file contains both made-up lists for October.

Download the practice file

Excel file with made-up data. No sign-up.

Step 1: ask what each total counts

Write both definitions side by side before comparing any numbers.

QuestionWhy it matters
Which date decides the month?Order, delivery, invoice and posting dates can fall in different months
Are cancelled orders included?An order report may keep them
How are returns and credit notes shown?One report may take them off and the other may not
Gross or net of discounts?Discounts may be agreed or recorded at different points
With or without sales tax and other charges?The same invoice can show two amounts
Which companies, branches and currencies?The two reports may not cover the same business

A sales report often exists to manage selling activity. Finance figures follow accounting policies. For example, under the international standard IFRS 15, revenue is recognised when a performance obligation is satisfied, which is when the customer gains control of the goods or service. So do not assume that an order date or a report date decides the accounting month. Ask your finance team which rules and policies apply.

Step 2: freeze both lists

Export both on the same day for the same scope, and keep untouched copies. If either system changes while you work, your explanation will stop adding up.

Step 3: match invoice by invoice

First check that each invoice number appears only once in each list, or add up the lines for each invoice. In the practice file, credit notes have their own numbers. On the Sales report sheet, put this in F2 and copy it down to find each invoice’s October finance amount:

=IF(COUNTIF('Finance ledger'!$A$2:$A$30,A2)=0,"Not in finance",SUMIFS('Finance ledger'!$D$2:$D$30,'Finance ledger'!$A$2:$A$30,A2,'Finance ledger'!$B$2:$B$30,">="&DATE(2026,10,1),'Finance ledger'!$B$2:$B$30,"<="&DATE(2026,10,31)))

Then check the other direction, so entries that exist only in finance are not missed. On the Finance ledger sheet, put this in F2 and copy it down:

=IF(COUNTIF('Sales report'!$A$2:$A$30,A2)=0,"Not in sales report","")

Step 4: give every difference a name

In the practice file, 11 of the 14 invoices in the sales report show the same amount in finance for October. The other three, together with two entries that appear only in finance, explain the whole gap.

ItemReferenceEffect on the bridgeWho confirms it
Cancelled order still in the sales reportS-7011−50,000Sales
Invoice posted by finance in NovemberS-7012−80,000Finance
Credit note for returned goodsCN-901−30,000Finance and sales
Full discount agreed at billing, not in sales reportS-7014−20,000Sales
Entry in finance that the sales report does not haveS-7015+15,000Both teams

Step 5: build the bridge

A bridge starts at one total, lists each named difference and must end exactly at the other: 1,250,000 − 50,000 − 80,000 − 30,000 − 20,000 + 15,000 = 1,085,000. If anything is left over, show it as not yet explained. Do not hide it in an “other” or “adjustment” line.

Put the sales total in B1, the five differences in B2 to B6 with their signs, and the finance total in B7. This cell should show zero:

=B1+SUM(B2:B6)-B7

A zero shows that the arithmetic closes. It does not prove that each explanation is right, so keep the evidence for every line.

Step 6: decide what to fix

  • Timing differences: note which month each report will count, and check that the item appears there.
  • Definition differences: label both reports clearly, or agree one definition for management meetings.
  • Errors: correct them in the system of record through the proper process, not only in your spreadsheet.
  • Repeating causes: give one owner and a date, and record the action.

A short monthly routine

  1. Agree the export date and scope.
  2. Run the two matching checks.
  3. Update the bridge and list unexplained amounts.
  4. Review open items with both teams.
  5. Save the bridge with its evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Which total is the correct one?

It depends on the question. For accounts, follow finance and the applicable accounting policies. For another purpose, agree the definition and label the report with it.

Is a small difference acceptable?

Agree a tolerance only with the people responsible, and remember that two large errors can cancel each other into a small difference.

Can I use XLOOKUP instead?

You can in versions that have it. Microsoft’s page says it is not available in Excel 2016 or 2019. A lookup also returns only one match, so check for repeated invoice numbers first.

References

  • IFRS Foundation: IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers. Overview page, including when a performance obligation is satisfied. It is not the full standard, and the page does not discuss order or invoice dates.
  • Microsoft Support: SUMIFS function. How to add values that meet several conditions.
  • Microsoft Support: XLOOKUP function. Lookup behaviour and version availability.
