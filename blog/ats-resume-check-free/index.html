How to Check Your Resume Against a Job Description for Free

A step-by-step way to compare your resume with a job advert, understand your ATS match score, and fix real keyword gaps without stuffing or exaggerating.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 5 November 20265 min read

Sending one resume to every job is quick, but it often leaves out the skills an employer is looking for, even when you have them. A simple check takes about 20 minutes per application: compare your resume with the job advert, find the gaps, and fix only those that truthfully apply to you. Here is how to do that for free, and how to read the results sensibly.

What you need

  • Your resume as plain text. Open your document, select everything, and copy it.
  • The full job advert, including the responsibilities and requirements. If it might disappear, save a copy.
  • The free ATS Resume Checker. It runs in your browser, so nothing you paste is uploaded.

Step 1: Run the first check

Paste your resume into the first box and the job description into the second, then select Analyse match. You will need at least about 30 words of resume and 20 words of job description. You will then see a match score, a verdict, a list of missing and matched keywords, and a set of structure checks.

Step 2: Understand how the score works

The score is out of 100 and has two parts:

PartWeightWhat it looks at
Keyword match70%Up to 25 of the most frequent meaningful words in the advert. Words repeated more often count for more.
Structure and quality30%Email, phone number, Experience, Education and Skills headings, length of 250 to 1,000 words, at least 5 numbers, at least 4 action verbs, and no table characters.

The verdict is Strong match at 75 or above, Moderate match from 55 to 74, and Needs work below 55.

For example, suppose the words you match make up 60% of the keyword weight, and you earn 20 of the 23 structure points. Your score is (0.6 × 70) + (20 ÷ 23 × 30) = 68, a moderate match. Lifting the keyword share to 80% would bring it to 82.

Keep the score in perspective. It's a guide built on keywords and formatting. It isn't the method of any real applicant tracking system, and employers use many different systems. A high score can't promise an interview, and a lower one doesn't mean you shouldn't apply.

Step 3: Sort the missing keywords

This is the most useful step. The tool picks words by how often they appear, so some will matter and others won't. Sort the missing list into four groups:

GroupExampleWhat to do
You have it and said it differentlyThe advert says "stakeholder management"; you wrote "worked with departments"Use the employer's wording
You have it and left it outYou use Power BI but didn't mention itAdd it with an example of how you used it
You don't have itThe advert asks for SAP, and you haven't used itLeave it out, and mention any related experience honestly in your cover letter
It isn't a real skillThe company's name, a city, or general words such as "business"Ignore it

Step 4: Know the tool's blind spots

Like any simple checker, it can flag a word even when you have covered the idea:

  • Word forms: if the advert says "managing" and you wrote "managed", it may be listed as missing. Don't rewrite a good sentence just to satisfy the tool.
  • Abbreviations: "KPI" and "key performance indicators" are separate to the checker. Spelling it out once, as "Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)", covers both forms.
  • Synonyms: it can't tell that "recruitment" and "hiring" mean nearly the same thing, so you need to judge these yourself.
  • Meaning: it only checks that a word appears. Mentioning "budgeting" isn't the same as showing you managed a budget, and only a person can judge how strong your evidence is.

Step 5: Put keywords where they count

A list of keywords at the bottom might raise your score, but it won't persuade a recruiter. Put each one where it shows real experience:

  • Summary: your job title and the two or three skills that matter most for this role.
  • Skills: specific tools and methods that you can discuss at interview.
  • Experience: the strongest place, because it shows the skill in action.

Here's a before and after, using made-up details:

BeforeAfter
Worked with different departments on reportsLed stakeholder management across sales, finance and supply chain for monthly performance reports
Made dashboards for managersBuilt Power BI dashboards that gave 15 managers a weekly view of sales against target

Use only details that are true for you, because you may be asked to explain them at interview.

Step 6: Fix the structure warnings

  • Missing email or phone: place them in the main body of the page, near the top.
  • Missing headings: use plain labels such as "Experience", "Education" and "Skills".
  • Too short or too long: below 250 words, add more about your achievements. Above 1,000, cut older or less relevant detail.
  • Too few numbers: add team sizes, volumes, time saved or results, wherever you honestly can.
  • Too few action verbs: begin bullets with words such as "Led", "Improved" or "Delivered".
  • Table characters: rebuild the resume in a simple single-column layout. The Resume Builder does this for you.

Step 7: Check again, then stop

Paste in your edited resume and run it once more. Then read it as a recruiter would. If a sentence now sounds forced, restore it, even if your score drops a little. A natural, honest resume with a score of 70 is better than a stuffed one at 95.

Save each version under a clear name, such as "Firstname-Lastname-Sales-Analyst.pdf", so you know which version went to which employer.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Chasing 100: matching every word usually means copying the advert. Your resume should sound like you.
  • Hiding keywords: white or tiny text can show up when a recruiter views the extracted text, which can cost you their trust.
  • Claiming skills you don't have: it might get you past a filter, but it won't get you through an interview.
  • Stopping at the score: your application still needs a clear summary, and often a good cover letter.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ATS score?

On this tool, 75 or above is labelled a strong match and 55 to 74 a moderate one. There is no universal standard, because other tools and real tracking systems work differently.

Is the resume checker really free?

Yes. There's no sign-up, and your text stays in your browser.

Can I upload a PDF or Word file?

No. Paste your resume as plain text. Copying from a PDF is also a useful test: if the text comes out jumbled, an employer's system may struggle with it too.

Will a high score get me an interview?

No score can promise that. It only shows that your resume shares language with the advert and has a sound structure. Your experience and how well it fits the role matter most.
