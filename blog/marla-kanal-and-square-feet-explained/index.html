Marla, Kanal and Square Feet Explained

How marla and kanal are measured, why a marla is not always the same size, how to convert to square feet and compare plot prices fairly before you buy.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 20 November 20265 min read

If you have ever looked at plots in Pakistan, you have met the marla and the kanal. They are traditional units of land area that remain the standard in property adverts, society files and everyday conversation. They are convenient, but they can also confuse, because a marla is not the same size everywhere. Misunderstanding the unit can cost real money. This guide explains what the units mean, how to convert them, and how to compare prices fairly.

The basics: marla and kanal

A kanal is the larger unit and a marla is the smaller one. The common relationship is that one kanal equals 20 marla. Beyond that, the actual area depends on which marla is meant. Pakistan has two widely used values:

  • The standard (revenue) marla: 272.25 square feet, which is 30.25 square yards. This comes from older land records.
  • The housing-society marla: often 225 square feet, which is 25 square yards. Many modern housing schemes use this smaller size.

So a plot described as 5 marla could be 1,361.25 square feet or 1,125 square feet, depending on the scheme and its file. That is a difference of over 236 square feet, roughly the size of a small room.

Converting to square feet

The conversion is simple multiplication. Area in square feet equals the number of marla times the size of one marla.

  • 5 marla at 272.25 sq ft = 1,361.25 sq ft.
  • 5 marla at 225 sq ft = 1,125 sq ft.
  • 1 kanal at 272.25 sq ft per marla = 20 x 272.25 = 5,445 sq ft.
  • 1 kanal at 225 sq ft per marla = 20 x 225 = 4,500 sq ft.

To go the other way, divide the square feet by the size of one marla. A plot of 2,250 square feet is 10 marla on a 225 sq ft marla, or about 8.26 marla on a 272.25 sq ft marla.

Always check the marla size

Before you discuss price, find out the exact plot dimensions in feet or yards on the file, map or allotment letter. Look for the length and width, not just the marla label. Multiply length by width to get the area, and then compare with the stated marla size. If the numbers do not match, ask for an explanation. Also remember that sizes written on a file may differ slightly from what is on the ground, so a survey is wise for large purchases.

Comparing prices fairly

A lower price per marla does not always mean a better deal, because the marlas may be different sizes. The fair way to compare is price per square foot. Divide the total price by the area in square feet. Consider two offers: Plot A is 10 marla at 225 sq ft each, priced at 15,000,000 rupees. Plot B is 10 marla at 272.25 sq ft each, priced at 17,500,000. Plot A has 2,250 sq ft, so it costs about 6,667 rupees per square foot. Plot B has 2,722.5 sq ft, so it costs about 6,428 rupees per square foot. Plot B is the cheaper option per square foot even though its headline price is higher.

Beyond the plot price

The advertised price is rarely the full cost. Budget for transfer fees, stamp duty, agent commission, development and utility charges, boundary work, and construction. The Pakistan Property Price Converter lets you add extra costs as a percentage, shows the price per marla and per square foot, and converts the total to dirhams, riyals, pounds and dollars, which helps overseas buyers who earn in other currencies. Exchange rates it uses are mid-market rates, so real transfers cost slightly more because banks and exchange services add margins.

Other units you will meet

  • Square yard (gaz): 9 square feet. Common in some cities for plot sizes.
  • Acre: 43,560 square feet, or 8 kanal at 5,445 sq ft per kanal.
  • Square metre: about 10.764 square feet, used in official and international documents.
  • Sarsai and others: regional units that vary. Ask for conversion to square feet in writing.

The Unit Converter covers Pakistani land and weight units as well as length, volume, temperature and more.

Common mistakes buyers make

  • Assuming a marla is always 272.25 square feet. Check the scheme's rule.
  • Comparing price per marla across different areas without converting to a common unit.
  • Relying on verbal sizes. Get the measurements written on documents.
  • Ignoring extra costs, which can add several percent to the price.
  • Skipping verification. Confirm ownership, approvals and boundaries with the relevant authority and a qualified lawyer.

Planning the finance

If you plan to borrow to buy or build, work out the monthly instalment before you commit. Our guides on the loan EMI formula and on flat rate versus reducing balance explain how lenders calculate repayments, and the monthly budget guide helps you check that the payment fits.

Short summary

  • 1 kanal = 20 marla.
  • A marla is 272.25 or 225 square feet depending on the source.
  • Convert everything to square feet before comparing prices.
  • Use written dimensions, not only the marla label.
  • Add transfer, agent and development costs to the budget.
