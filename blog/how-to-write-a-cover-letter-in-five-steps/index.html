How to Write a Cover Letter in Five Steps

A clear five-step method for a cover letter that gets read: research, opening, evidence, fit and closing, plus common mistakes and a structure to follow.

By Gmd Khan Niazi (Ghulam Mohiuddin Khan Niazi)Published 20 November 20265 min read

A cover letter has one job: to make the reader want to open your CV and invite you to an interview. Many people treat it as a formality, copying a template and changing the company name. Recruiters notice, and generic letters are the first to be skipped. A good letter is short, specific and written for one job. This guide gives you a five-step method that works for fresh graduates and experienced professionals alike.

Before you write: read the job advert like a detective

Print or copy the advert and underline three things: the main responsibilities, the must-have skills and any words that describe the working style, such as fast-paced, detail-oriented or customer-focused. Then look at the company. What does it do, who are its customers, and what recent news or products can you mention truthfully? Ten minutes of research gives you the raw material for a letter that sounds written for them.

Step 1: Address the right person

If the advert names a person, use their name. If it does not, a quick search of the company's page or professional network may reveal the hiring manager or head of the department. When you truly cannot find a name, "Dear Hiring Manager" is acceptable. Avoid "To whom it may concern", which sounds distant, and avoid errors in the company name, which are an instant red flag.

Step 2: Open with the role and one reason

Your first paragraph should state the job you are applying for and give one strong reason why you are a fit. Do not begin with a long story about yourself. For example: "I am applying for the Sales Officer role at your Lahore branch. In my current role I grew territory sales by 18 percent in six months, and I would like to bring the same results to your team." Two sentences, specific and relevant.

Step 3: Give evidence, not adjectives

Words like hardworking, motivated and passionate prove nothing. Replace them with facts. Choose two or three achievements that match what the advert asks for, and describe each in one or two sentences with a number where possible: what you did, how you did it, what changed. If you are a graduate, use projects, internships, volunteering or university work. A third-year project that you led for a team of four is evidence. Be honest, since every claim can be checked at the interview.

Step 4: Show why this company

This is the paragraph that separates a tailored letter from a template. Say what attracts you to this employer and connect it to your strengths. It might be their product range, their approach to training, or a project they ran. Keep it genuine and specific: one sentence about them, one about how you can contribute.

Step 5: Close with a clear next step

End confidently and politely. Thank the reader, say you have attached your CV, and state that you would welcome the chance to discuss the role. Use a simple sign-off such as "Kind regards" and your full name, phone number and email. Keep the whole letter to about 250 to 350 words, or about one page.

A structure you can copy

  1. Greeting: Dear Ms Aisha Khan,
  2. Paragraph 1 (role and hook): the job title and your strongest relevant result.
  3. Paragraph 2 (evidence): two achievements that match the advert.
  4. Paragraph 3 (fit): why this company and how you will help.
  5. Paragraph 4 (close): thanks, attached CV, availability for an interview.
  6. Sign-off: name and contact details.

A worked example

Take a candidate applying for an operations coordinator job. The advert stresses reporting accuracy, vendor coordination and Excel skills. The opening names the role and mentions that the candidate reduced reporting errors by building a checked Excel template. The evidence paragraph gives two examples: coordinating eight vendors to cut delivery delays, and producing a weekly dashboard used by management. The fit paragraph mentions that the company is expanding to new cities and that the candidate has experience setting up processes for growth. The close thanks the reader and offers to bring sample reports to the interview. Each paragraph answers something the advert asked.

Mistakes that cost interviews

  • Repeating the CV. The letter should add context, not list the same facts.
  • Being too long. More than one page is rarely read.
  • Using the same letter everywhere. Recruiters can tell. Change the opening and the fit paragraph for every application.
  • Spelling and grammar errors. Read it aloud and ask someone to check it.
  • Overclaiming. If you cannot back a claim at the interview, leave it out.
  • Negativity. Do not criticise a previous employer.

The more real detail you provide, the better the draft.

Final checklist

  • Named the right person and company.
  • Stated the job title in the first paragraph.
  • Included two or three specific achievements.
  • Explained why this company.
  • Kept it to one page and proofread it.
  • Saved it as a PDF with a clear file name, such as Ali-Khan-Cover-Letter.pdf.
