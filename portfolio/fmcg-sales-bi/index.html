What a manager could check next

  1. Sample Distributor D has the lowest achievement at 84.0%, a gap of 276,300 against a total gap of 488,700. First check: outlet coverage (70.6% against 76.1% overall).
  2. Sample Distributor B has about 7 days of stock. Confirm pending orders before outlets run out.
  3. Sample Distributor G has about 31 days of stock. Review its selling plan before sending more.
  4. Beverages is the weakest category at 94.6% of target. Check availability by distributor before changing the target.

These are questions the sample figures raise. Data like this shows where to look; it does not prove why something happened.

How the Excel automation works

  • New rows go into two data sheets, Sales Data and Outlet Data. The dashboard is not typed by hand.
  • Totals use SUMIFS by month, distributor and category. Changing the month cell recalculates the tables.
  • Ratios use IFERROR, so an empty period shows zero instead of an error.
  • The Checks sheet compares dashboard totals with the source rows and shows “Matched” or “CHECK”.
  • The workbook has no macros and no links to outside files.

When the data sheets grow past their current rows, the formula ranges need extending or converting to Excel tables. A delivered project sets this up for your file.

Questions

Is this a real client dashboard?

No. Every figure is invented with a fixed random seed to demonstrate the method. The distributor names are placeholders.

Can I check the formulas?

Yes. Download the workbook. The dashboard uses SUMIFS formulas on two data sheets, and a Checks sheet compares totals with the source rows.

Can this be built on my data?

Usually, if your export has a stable layout. Send a sample with private details removed, and the scope and price are confirmed in writing first.

Are the 10 and 25 day stock flags a standard?

No. They are example limits. Suitable stock cover depends on your products, delivery times and shelf life.
